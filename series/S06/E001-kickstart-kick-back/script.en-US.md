## why-kickback

**VO:**
- Have you ever wondered why a dirt bike can **kick** you **back** — when you're the one kicking it?
- It's not the engine starting backward. It's the crank losing a split-second **tug-of-war** just before top dead center.

## foot-to-piston

**VO:** Press the lever: the **starter gears** turn the crank, and the crank drives the piston **upward**, compressing the mixture.

## spark-before-tdc

**VO:** A spark just before top dead center is **normal** — the flame needs **time** to build.

## pressure-after-tdc

**VO:** With enough **momentum**, the crank crosses the top, pressure peaks **after** it, and the engine keeps turning forward.

## too-early

**VO:**
- But if pressure builds **too soon**, the crank pin is still on the approaching side.
- Combustion pushes the piston down. The connecting rod pushes that offset pin at an angle, and creates **reverse torque**.

## forward-stop-reverse

**VO:** The crank **slows**, **stops**, then **reverses** a few degrees.

## force-travels-back

**VO:** Because the starter gears are still **engaged**, that force travels back through the kickshaft and lever — straight into your **boot**.

## brief-reverse

**VO:**
- That's kickback: a **brief reversal**, then a stall — not an engine running backward.
- Being in gear isn't the main cause. If it keeps happening, check the **ignition timing**, the decompressor, and your starting technique.
