# Brief — S03E003 barber-track-day-three

Day three of the 100-track mission, in Tonglu — a small town an hour out of Hangzhou. The
track is found through its **barber**: the man cutting your hair on a weekday runs the
local track on the weekend. The episode's argument is that tracks like this are not
listed anywhere, and the only way to find them is to show up and talk to people.

## Viewer (one concrete person)

A rider who has already searched for tracks online and come up with a map of nothing — and
who assumes that means there is nothing near them. What they actually lack is the local
who knows.

## Pain point, in the viewer's words

- en-US: "There's nothing near me — I've looked." (The tracks are there; they're in
  someone's head, not on a map.)
- zh-CN: "附近哪有什么场地，我找过了。" A separately authored zh-CN read is recorded and cut
  (VO_ZH_005) — the second in S03 after E002.5, and the first full-length one. It leans
  harder on Hangzhou dialect (木佬佬, 来赛) than the en does on any register, which is a
  deliberate local-colour choice, not a translation artefact.

## One-sentence viewer benefit (the core conclusion this video proves)

The tracks that never make it onto a map are found by people, not search — and the app is
where one person's local knowledge becomes everyone's.

## Topic formula (one line)

Pain (nothing near me is on any map) + solution (the track is found through the barber who
runs it) + benefit (local knowledge becomes a pin anyone can ride to).

The haircut is the structural joke: `but-first` reads as a non-sequitur for exactly one
beat, and `the-barber` turns it into the premise.
